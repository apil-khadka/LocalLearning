Force and motion - original demonstration note
Grade 8 / Science / Force and Motion

This is a short demonstration note, not an official curriculum textbook.

A force is a push or a pull. The resultant force on an object is the combined effect of all forces acting on it. If those forces balance, the object does not accelerate.

Newton's second law relates resultant force, mass and acceleration: F = m × a. Force is measured in newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg), and acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²).

Suppose a cart has a mass of 4 kg. A resultant horizontal force of 8 N acts on the cart. Its acceleration is a = F / m = 8 / 4 = 2 m/s². If the mass doubles while the same resultant force acts, acceleration becomes 1 m/s². This calculation treats the stated force as the resultant force after friction and any other forces have been combined.

A loaded cart needs more resultant force than an empty cart to reach the same acceleration. This is because the loaded cart has more mass.

Check your understanding: What resultant force is needed to accelerate a 3 kg cart at 2 m/s²? Explain your reasoning using the relationship between force, mass and acceleration.
